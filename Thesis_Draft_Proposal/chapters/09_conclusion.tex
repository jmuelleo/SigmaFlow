\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis replaced the score-based diffusion mechanism of a rigid-fragment
protein--ligand docking model with Riemannian flow matching on
$(\SEthree)^N$, holding the equivariant backbone, the data pipeline and the
computational budget fixed so that the substitution itself could be measured.

Three results are established independently of the longer training runs. The
substitution simplifies the model's interface, replacing a score that requires
noise-dependent rescaling with a velocity that does not. It permits a fivefold
reduction in integration steps at no measurable cost in accuracy, the
signature expected of near-straight conditional paths. And it exposes an
asymmetry between the factors of the state space: at matched budget the
translational channel carries signal while the rotational channel is
statistically indistinguishable from uniform guessing.

The principal theoretical contribution is a candidate explanation for that
asymmetry. The geodesic conditional target on $\SOthree$ is independent of
time (Proposition~\ref{prop:t-invariance}), so flow matching demands the
complete rotational answer at every $t$, including $t=0$ where the state is
uninformative; the $L^2$-optimal response is then a contracting conditional
mean. Translation escapes this because its source is centred on the binding
pocket and therefore informative, whereas the Haar measure has no distinguished
orientation to hedge towards. Diffusion's noise schedule supplies, incidentally,
the graded curriculum that the flow-matching construction discards.

Beyond docking, the analysis suggests a general point. When a conditional
generative model is built on a homogeneous space, the invariant measure is the
obvious source distribution and is often the only one considered --- but
invariance is precisely the property that makes it uninformative. Flow
matching, unlike diffusion, leaves that choice open. Whether exploiting it
repairs the rotational channel here is the question the remaining experiments
are designed to answer.
