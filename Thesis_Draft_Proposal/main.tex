\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Molecular docking predicts the bound geometry of a small molecule in a protein
binding site. Recent generative approaches cast this as sampling from a
conditional distribution over rigid-body poses, a space that is not Euclidean:
a pose is an element of a product of special Euclidean groups. SigmaDock
attacks this with score-based diffusion. This thesis replaces that generative
mechanism with Riemannian flow matching while holding the network
architecture, the data pipeline, and the computational budget fixed, so that
the substitution itself --- rather than a confounded collection of changes ---
is what is measured.

The resulting system, SigmaFlow, is compared to its diffusion parent at
matched training compute. Early experiments reveal a sharp asymmetry between
the two factors of the state space: the translational channel carries signal
while the rotational channel is, at this budget, statistically
indistinguishable from uniform guessing. We derive a candidate mechanism from
the geometry of the construction. The geodesic conditional path on
$\SOthree$ has a regression target that is \emph{independent of time}, so flow
matching demands the complete answer at $t=0$, where it is not identifiable
from the state; the $L^2$-optimal response is then a conditional mean of
rotation vectors, which contracts. Translation escapes this because its source
distribution is informative and rotation's, being Haar, is not.
