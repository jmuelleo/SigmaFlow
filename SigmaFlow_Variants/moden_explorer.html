Two thresholds that both happen to be 2 Å. The clustering threshold decides how similar two poses must be to count as the same binding mode — a modelling choice, and adjustable above. The success criterion decides how close a pose must be to the crystal structure to count as correct — the field standard from PoseBusters. They are independent, and pushing them apart is instructive: at a 1 Å success criterion much of what passes at 2 Å turns out to have individual fragments rotated the wrong way.

How the modes are defined. Pairwise RMSD without superposition — the poses already sit in the protein frame, so superimposing them would destroy the spatial information at issue — then complete-linkage hierarchical clustering. Complete linkage merges two groups only once their largest cross distance falls below the threshold, which guarantees what the eye expects: every pair of poses inside a mode lies within the threshold of every other. Average linkage does not give that guarantee. Modes are numbered by size, so Mode 1 is always the largest. RMSD to the crystal pose is symmetry-corrected.

What to look for. A large mode is not automatically the right one. Where the ranker succeeds, it sometimes selects from a mode holding a quarter of the samples while the largest mode holds none that are correct.
